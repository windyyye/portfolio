> margin: No. 001 — the entry where the site changes its mind.

This site changed its mind before it ever launched.

![A wobbly signpost pointing two ways](/images/pivot-signpost.svg)

The version still smelling of paint was a portfolio. It had a spec written to win freelance clients, offer cards for frontend / QA / agentic builds, a "how I work" pitch, and a definition of done that went: *"a stranger knows within 30 seconds what Windy offers."* I built it fast — Astro, Firebase, a custom domain — and I was happy with it. But most of the pages were empty shells, and something more interesting had already started happening in the repo.

Meanwhile I'd opened an issue titled just "revamp" that said the quiet part out loud:

> the objective has changed. instead of focusing on the portfolio, take it as a project to explore interesting designs a website/portfolio can be, reflecting on my personality. it acts almost like a digital diary / journal book that narrows more into my career.

> margin: "for fun, basically." — issue #21, verbatim

And then, lower down, the reason: *this project is personal, not intended for marketing purposes nor showcases. for fun, basically.*

So that's what this is now. Not a shop window — a sketchbook that narrows into my career, kept in public because public is training: it makes me get things done properly and professionally, with modules and weeks of preparation. Self-discipline, and a rehearsal for real-world projects.

What survives from v1: the honesty rules, the public repo, agents doing the building under direction while I spec and review. What doesn't: the offer cards are gone, the contact funnel is gone, and the stranger with 30 seconds is free to wander. The old portfolio isn't deleted anyway — it's history, and this entry is where it turns into a story.

The new look came from an archetype sweep — fifteen sites, my reactions recorded as findings. I loved the illustrated garden. I liked book craft but wanted color. I loved neo-brutalism right up until it hurt my eyes. What you're reading is what fell out of that: a diary wearing book clothes — enough book to feel authored, not so much that it stops being fun — colored softly, drawn by hand.

House rules for Vol. I, written down so future-me can't weasel out of them: every image traces to something real; almost nothing moves; the whole thing costs zero; and entries are allowed to stay unfinished — this one ships as a seedling, and seedlings are allowed to stay seedlings.

This is entry No. 001. The pivot goes first because the pivot is the truest thing this site has.
